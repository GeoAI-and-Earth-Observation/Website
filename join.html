Prospective students and researchers

Join the research group

Information for students interested in GeoAI, Earth observation, critical infrastructure, robotics, and spatial computing research at George Mason University.

Research fit

Who should consider getting in touch?

We are particularly interested in students who want to work at the intersection of geospatial science, computation, and real-world systems. Current research spans three broad areas.

  1. Critical infrastructure modeling and simulation for energy, transportation, and telecommunications networks, including resilience, risk, and decision support.
  2. Earth observation using optical imagery, Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR), lidar, and multisensor remote sensing, with an emphasis on scalable geospatial analysis and GeoAI.
  3. Robotics and spatial computing, particularly the geospatial and computational challenges involved in enabling autonomous systems to perceive, interpret, and navigate the built environment.
Before contacting us

Help us understand your research fit.

Prospective students should first review the group’s research themes and recent publications. When emailing, please include enough information for us to assess potential alignment.

Please include

  • Your CV or résumé
  • Your current degree, institution, and expected graduation date
  • A short description of your research interests
  • Relevant programming, GIS, remote sensing, modeling, or robotics experience
  • Why you are specifically interested in this research group
  • For PhD inquiries, the approximate semester or year you hope to begin

Frequently asked questions

Prospective student FAQ

This page provides general guidance. Specific opportunities and funding depend on active projects, university admissions, and available research support.

Are you accepting PhD students?

We welcome inquiries from prospective PhD students whose interests closely align with the group’s research. Availability varies by year and by funded project, so an inquiry does not imply that a funded position is currently open.

Do you have funded PhD positions or research assistantships?

Funding opportunities depend on active grants, departmental support, and the timing of individual projects. When a specific funded position is available, details can be added to this page. Strong prospective students are still welcome to contact us to discuss research fit and possible pathways.

Can Master's students join the group?

Yes. Master's students may become involved through thesis or project work, research assistantships when available, independent research, or collaboration with ongoing group projects.

Can undergraduate students get involved?

Yes. Motivated undergraduates can contribute to suitable research tasks, particularly where they have or are developing skills in Python, GIS, remote sensing, data analysis, machine learning, or related computational methods.

What technical skills are useful?

Useful preparation includes Python, GIS, geospatial data processing, statistics, machine learning, remote sensing, network analysis, simulation, cloud or high-performance computing, and reproducible research workflows. The exact skill set depends on the project.

Do I need previous GeoAI or remote sensing experience?

Not necessarily. Strong quantitative, computational, engineering, or geospatial foundations can transfer well. We care about research potential, technical curiosity, and evidence that you can learn the methods needed for a project.

Can I propose my own research topic?

Potentially. Student-led ideas are welcome when they connect clearly with the group’s expertise and can be developed into a tractable research question. PhD projects generally benefit from substantial overlap with active faculty research and available data, methods, and funding.

What software and methods does the group use?

Projects commonly involve Python and open-source geospatial tools, GIS, Earth observation processing, machine learning and computer vision, spatial network analysis, simulation, and scalable computing. Individual projects may use additional domain-specific software or hardware.

Are there opportunities to publish and attend conferences?

Research is undertaken with the aim of producing rigorous, publishable scholarship where appropriate. Conference participation and publication opportunities depend on the maturity of the work, available funding, and each student’s contribution.

Should I contact the group before applying to GMU?

For prospective PhD students, contacting us before applying can be useful when there is a strong research match. Formal admission, however, is handled through George Mason University's relevant academic program and is separate from an informal research-group conversation.

What makes a strong introductory email?

A strong email is short and specific. Explain the research problem that interests you, why it connects with our work, the relevant skills or experience you already have, and what kind of opportunity you are seeking. Attach a CV and avoid generic messages sent to many faculty members.
